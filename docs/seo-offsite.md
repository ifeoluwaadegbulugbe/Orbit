# Off-Page SEO: Backlinks & Listings

Legitimate backlink and listing opportunities, prioritized. No paid link schemes, no link farms, no spam.

## Priority 1: Software directories

These are high-intent (people searching them are already evaluating tools) and typically free or low-cost to list on.

- **G2** — Service/salon software and CRM categories. Requires a verified company profile.
- **Capterra** — Appointment scheduling and salon software categories.
- **GetApp** — Same parent company as Capterra, separate listing.
- **SaaSHub** — Free listing, good for early-stage tools.
- **AlternativeTo** — List Orbit as an alternative to WhatsApp/spreadsheet-based booking workflows. (Fresha/Booksy-specific positioning was dropped, see `content/TODO-verify.md`: the "flat, never a commission" claim it rested on turned out to be inaccurate.)

**Pitch template (adapt per directory's submission form):**
> Orbit is a booking, invoicing, and client management platform built for African service businesses, nail technicians, hairstylists, photographers, makeup artists, and barbers. Pro is a flat $12/month subscription regardless of volume, and every booking requires the owner's approval before it's confirmed.

## Priority 2: Launch platforms

- **Product Hunt** — A single, well-prepared launch day (not a passive listing) tends to drive the most value here. Needs a maker account, a crisp one-line description, and ideally a short demo video or GIF showing the booking-approval flow, the clearest differentiator to show visually.
- **Crunchbase** — A company profile here supports credibility and is often checked by press and partners, low effort to set up.

## Priority 3: African tech and startup media

Target publications covering African startups and fintech/SaaS specifically. Before pitching, confirm current submission guidelines and preferred contact method on each outlet's own site, since these change.

**Pitch angle:** lead with the owner-approval booking flow (a concrete, demoable differentiator) rather than a generic "we built a CRM" pitch, which is a crowded, less newsworthy framing.

## Priority 4: Community and creator partnerships

- Beauty and creative-industry community groups (Facebook groups, WhatsApp communities, Instagram community accounts) that already serve the target audience (nail techs, stylists, photographers). A partnership here is less about a backlink and more about trusted referral traffic; a backlink from a community page is a bonus, not the main goal.
- Micro-influencer partnerships with beauty/creative professionals who'd genuinely use the product, structured as an honest review or walkthrough rather than a paid link placement, since undisclosed paid links violate most platforms' guidelines and can be penalized by search engines.

## What to avoid

- Paid link placements on low-quality "guest post" sites whose only purpose is selling links.
- Private blog networks (PBNs) or link farms.
- Reciprocal link exchanges done purely for SEO value rather than genuine relevance.
- Directory submissions to irrelevant, low-quality directories just to accumulate backlink count.

## Tracking

Keep a simple log (spreadsheet or a `leads`-style table) of: where pitched, date, contact, response, outcome (listed / declined / no response), and whether a backlink actually went live. Follow up once after 2-3 weeks of silence, then move on.
