## Two different ways booking software makes money

Before comparing specific tools, it helps to understand that there are really only two pricing philosophies in booking software: marketplace pricing and flat subscription pricing. Almost every tool you'll come across fits into one of these two categories, and which one suits you depends less on features and more on how you actually get booked.

Marketplace platforms, which is broadly the category Fresha and Booksy fall into, combine a subscription with discovery features and, in various forms, fees tied to how the client pays or where they found you. The pitch is that the platform helps new clients discover your business, and in exchange, it takes a piece of that value, whether as a listing fee, a processing fee, or a commission on certain bookings.

Flat subscription tools, which is the category Orbit falls into, charge one predictable monthly fee regardless of how many bookings you take or how much revenue they represent. There's no commission on what you earn. The trade-off is that a flat-subscription tool generally doesn't try to bring you new clients through its own marketplace, it assumes you already have a way to reach clients and focuses on running the business behind that relationship well.

## Why this distinction matters more than feature lists

Most comparison articles focus on feature checklists: does it have reminders, does it support online payments, does it have a mobile app. In practice, most booking tools in this category have roughly similar core features. The number that actually determines your long-term cost is the pricing model underneath those features.

Here's why: a feature list doesn't scale with your success, but a commission does. A tool that takes even a small percentage of every booking costs you more in absolute terms every single month you get busier. A flat subscription costs the same whether you booked five clients or fifty.

## When marketplace pricing makes sense

Marketplace pricing earns its keep when discovery is genuinely valuable to you, meaning a meaningful share of your bookings would come from people searching the platform itself rather than finding you through your own channels. A new business with no existing following, operating in a market where that platform has strong local adoption, might find the trade-off worthwhile, at least while building an initial client base.

It's a reasonable starting point if you're truly starting from zero and have no other way to be discovered. The commission or fee you pay is, in effect, a customer acquisition cost.

## When flat subscription pricing makes more sense

Flat pricing makes more sense once you already have a client base that finds you without the platform's help, which describes a large share of African beauty, grooming, and creative service businesses. Clients are already following you on Instagram, messaging you on WhatsApp, or hearing about you from a friend. In that situation, paying a marketplace a cut of every booking buys you very little you don't already have.

## A simple way to work out which one costs you less

Do a rough calculation before deciding. Estimate how many bookings you take in a typical month and your average price per booking. If a marketplace platform's fees on that volume exceed a flat subscription fee, the flat model is cheaper for you at your current size, and it stays cheaper as you grow, since the flat fee doesn't increase with volume.

The one thing this calculation can't capture is how much of your current booking volume genuinely depends on marketplace discovery versus your own channels. Be honest with yourself here. If removing the marketplace tomorrow would barely dent your bookings, you're likely paying for discovery you don't need.

## It's not really about which tool is "better"

Fresha, Booksy, and Orbit aren't competing on the same axis. They're built around different assumptions about where your clients come from. The right choice isn't the one with the longest feature list, it's the one whose pricing model matches how you actually get booked.
